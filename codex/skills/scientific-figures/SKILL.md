description: Design, build, revise, and validate scientific figures when visual encoding, statistical correctness, units, print size, or editable source quality matters. Use for plots, schematics, diagrams, tables-as-figures, and paper-ready visual assets.
---

<scientific_figures>

<purpose>
Design a scientific figure around one visual proposition: what should a reader understand, and which evidence or relationship makes that possible? A correct asset collection or complete set of module boxes is not yet an explanatory figure. Preserve accepted visual baselines and the scientific claim boundary.
</purpose>

<design>
Infer a short figure brief from the manuscript and verified material: reader's question, indispensable content, likely misconception, available evidence and final physical size. Establish the focal subject, meaningful difference, hierarchy, grouping and reading order before choosing a renderer. A teaser expresses the work's distinctive relationship or capability; a method figure explains mechanism and learning; a result plot tests an empirical question. Avoid repeating the whole pipeline.

For consequential figures, inspect a few relevant reference figures and explain which organizational choices transfer to this task. When composition is unsettled, compare a small number of genuinely different low-cost layouts, not color variants. Select and refine the best-supported direction within the author's authorization; do not turn ordinary curves or local fixes into elaborate candidate reviews. Reference analysis and candidate choice are author-side work, separate from independent blind manuscript review.
</design>

<scientific_integrity>
Verify transformations, denominators, sampling units, replicate independence, axes, units, scales, thresholds, uncertainty and missing data against the source. Preserve failures and negative evidence. Distinguish asset availability, training exposure, held-out inputs and evaluated outcomes. Real geometry, contact, trajectories and experimental results must not be invented or repaired by image generation. Conceptual imagery may help explore composition when clearly distinguished from evidence.

Keep predictions, targets, losses, derivatives, ordinary data and parameter updates semantically distinct. Preserve source expressions and faithful mathematical typography. A loss symbol must show what is compared or learned; a more attractive diagram must not silently alter the method.
</scientific_integrity>

<production>
Separate evidence-bearing components from composition: data plots, geometry or recorded frames, mathematical expressions, and vector labels/connectors can have different production tools. Maintain one editing owner for each component so moving a label does not require regenerating a scene. Preserve data scripts, editable source, original media, asset identity and export settings. When a project needs organization, use topic directories with a declared master, paper export, current preview and local components; keep candidates and deprecated material distinct. Tool choice and editability do not establish visual quality.

Choose by need: Python for reproducible statistics, draw.io/XML for relational schematics, SVG/Inkscape for vector composition and local refinement, PPTX for an appropriate editable composition workflow, and 3D tools for geometry. Image generation is an option for intentional conceptual raster assets. Verify actual host capabilities and use the corresponding native tool guidance; do not require all these applications or copy their APIs into this skill. Keep compatibility claims conditional until the target export is tested.
</production>

<rendered_review>
Actually view each changed exported figure and the latest compiled pages at three scales: whole-page composition, normal final reading size, and enlarged details. First describe what a key figure says without relying on its caption, then compare that interpretation with its intended role and scientific text. A PDF path is only an input locator: rendered image content must reach vision inspection, through the PDF tools or an actual local-render viewing fallback.

Check focal subject, meaningful variation, hierarchy, grouping, whitespace, text/math typography, line/label collisions, arrow routes and endpoints, legends, cropping, masks, color accessibility and placement beside the argument. Compare revised key figures with the accepted baseline at the same size. Treat a failed visual proposition or ambiguous learning relationship as a revision need even without clipping. Inspect hats, subscripts and baselines after export. Check table captions against the actual template before judging convention, and separately judge whether a table or long caption earns its page space.

Reinspect the current figure, its compiled page and affected neighbors after substantive changes. A successful build, XML parser, source hash, inspection count or self-assigned aesthetic score is not a design verdict. Do not shrink everything to solve crowding or obscure negative results to improve appearance.
</rendered_review>

<autonomy_and_review_loop>
Within approved scope, choose references, layouts, tools, representative material and ordinary repairs autonomously. Resolve unverifiable scientific content from authorized evidence or mark the gap; do not silently invent it. When iterations cease to improve the result, preserve the stronger candidate and diagnose the unresolved composition or semantic problem rather than endlessly restyling it.

For substantial manuscript work or key-figure redesign, follow paper-writing's production, two independent focused reviews, revision and same-reviewer reinspection loop. Keep scientific and visual judgments visible separately. Do not declare an uninspected or rejected key figure finished, impose a defect quota, or equate agent checks with author visual acceptance.
</autonomy_and_review_loop>
